Lithium or tubular battery for solar: which is better?

By the SunGo Solar team, Gujranwala · 25 September 2026

Quick answer

Lithium batteries cost more at the start, but they last many more years, need no water or care, charge faster and let you use more of their power. Tubular (lead-acid) batteries are cheaper to buy but wear out sooner. For most solar homes, lithium is the better long-term choice.

The main differences

LithiumTubular (lead-acid)
Price at startHigherLower
LifeMany more yearsWears out sooner
MaintenanceNoneNeeds water top-up and care
Usable powerMost of its capacityAbout half, to protect the battery
Charging speedFastSlow
Weight and spaceLight, compactHeavy, needs more space

Why lithium usually costs less in the long run

A tubular battery may need replacing a few times during the life of one lithium battery. When you add up the replacements, the water and the lower usable power, lithium often works out cheaper over the years.

What do 12V, 24V and 51V mean?

This is the battery voltage. It must match your inverter. A 12V inverter needs a 12V battery, a 24V inverter needs a 24V battery, and a 48V inverter uses a 51V lithium battery (51V is the normal voltage of a 48V lithium battery). Bigger home systems usually use 51V batteries.

Common questions

More? See all FAQs.

Is lithium better than tubular battery for solar?

For most solar homes, yes. Lithium lasts longer, needs no maintenance and gives more usable power, even though it costs more at the start.

What does a 51V lithium battery mean?

51V is the normal voltage of a 48V lithium battery. It is used with 48V inverters, which are common in bigger home systems.

Can I use a 24V battery with a 48V inverter?

No. The battery voltage must match the inverter. Ask us and we will match the right battery to your inverter.
